Part 1 · Types of Chemical Reactions 10 pts

Read each equation, choose the type of reaction, then press Check.

Quick guide
  • Combination: two or more substances join to make one product (A + B → AB)
  • Decomposition: one compound breaks into simpler substances (AB → A + B)
  • Single Displacement: one element replaces another in a compound (A + BC → AC + B)
  • Double Displacement: two compounds swap partners (AB + CD → AD + CB)

Part 2 · Balancing Chemical Equations 10 pts

Type a coefficient from 1 to 6 in every box so the number of atoms of each element is equal on both sides. Use the smallest whole numbers. Use the scratch paper at the bottom of the page.

Example: H2 + O2 → H2O is balanced as 2 H2 + 1 O2 → 2 H2O (4 H and 2 O on each side). A coefficient of 1 is typed as 1.

Part 3 · Mole Ratios 10 pts

Look at the balanced equation and write the mole ratio of the two compounds named in each problem. Use the coefficients, in the same order as the question asks.

Guide: The coefficients of a balanced equation give the mole ratio. If no number is shown in front of a compound, its coefficient is 1. Equivalent ratios (for example 4 : 2 for 2 : 1) are accepted.
  • Example: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O
  • H2 to O2 = 2 : 1  ·  H2 to H2O = 2 : 2 (or 1 : 1)  ·  O2 to H2O = 1 : 2

Part 4 · Moles from Grams 10 pts

The mass of a compound is given in grams. Use its molecular mass to find the number of moles of the same compound. Round your answer to two decimal places.

Formula: moles = grams ÷ molecular mass (g/mol)
  • Example: 2H2 + O2 → 2H2O. Given 36.04 g of H2O (18.02 g/mol): 36.04 ÷ 18.02 = 2.00 mol H2O
